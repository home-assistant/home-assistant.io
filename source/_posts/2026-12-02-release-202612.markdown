## Home Assistant Cloud is now Home Assistant Link

Two months ago, [Nabu Casa](https://www.nabucasa.com/) announced that [Home Assistant Cloud is getting a new name](/blog/2026/10/02/big-tech-ruined-the-cloud-so-were-renaming-ours/). With this release, it happens: Home Assistant Cloud is now [Home Assistant Link](/link/). Same service, new name.

Why the change? Big Tech has given "the cloud" a bad name. Ever-rising subscription prices, outages, data harvesting, and devices that turn into paperweights the day their cloud is switched off. Home Assistant Link has never worked that way, but its name suggested it did. It even made some people think it was a way to run Home Assistant in the cloud, which it never was. Home Assistant runs in your home.

The new name describes what the service actually does: it links your local Home Assistant to a few optional online extras. Remote access when you are away from home. Alexa and Google Assistant without a weekend of setup. Off-site {% term backups %}, encrypted with a key that only you have. {% term STT "Speech-to-text" %} and {% term TTS "text-to-speech" %} for your voice assistants. And if you ever stop using it, your home keeps working, lights and all.

It is also how Home Assistant stays independent. Home Assistant is owned by the [Open Home Foundation](https://www.openhomefoundation.org/), a nonprofit, not by a company. Nabu Casa is a commercial partner of the foundation: it runs Home Assistant Link and makes the official Home Assistant hardware. Nabu Casa contributes the majority of the profits from subscriptions to the Open Home Foundation, which funds the development of Home Assistant. No investors, no advertising, and no selling your data. If you are curious about the full story, [the announcement by Carl Albertsson](/blog/2026/10/02/big-tech-ruined-the-cloud-so-were-renaming-ours/) is a great read.

In Home Assistant, you see the new name and a new icon in **Settings**, in the voice assistant setup, and everywhere else the service shows up. Some wording changed along with it: cloud backups are now called off-site backups.

There is nothing for you to do. Your subscription, remote access, voice, and backups keep working as they did. The integration still uses the `cloud` domain, so your {% term YAML %} configuration, {% term automations %}, and {% term scripts %} keep working too.

Thanks to the Nabu Casa team, and to everyone who shared their thoughts on the new name after the announcement! ❤️

## A cover dialog you can grab

Blinds, shutters, curtains, garage doors, gates: in Home Assistant, they are all {% term covers %}. And until now, they all got the same dialog, with an upside-down slider and a few buttons. That slider made sense for some covers, and was plain confusing for others. It never felt like you were controlling the thing hanging in front of your window.

So, we redesigned the cover dialog from scratch. It now shows a drawing of your cover, based on what kind of cover it is: a shutter looks like a shutter, a garage door like a garage door. And the drawing is the control. Tap it to send the cover to that position, or drag it to move it from where it is now. Covers that can only open and close do so with a swipe past halfway. Doors, windows, and dampers get a clean, simple panel instead, because Home Assistant also uses those types for things like pet doors and car windows.

<img src='/images/blog/2026-12/cover-more-info.png' alt='Screenshot of the new cover dialog of the office shutter, open at 25 percent. A drawing of the shutter shows its position, with blue sky in the open part at the bottom. Below it are Open, Stop, and Close buttons, and favorite positions of 0, 25, 75, and 100 percent.' class='no-shadow' />

Below the drawing are labeled **Open**, **Stop**, and **Close** buttons. Covers with tilt get a switch between position and tilt, just like the light dialog switches between brightness and color. And you can add your favorite positions, so "half open" is always one tap away.

The drawing only shows what your cover actually reports. While a cover is moving, the position you asked for is shown separately, until the cover gets there or stops. And for keyboard and screen reader users, the drawing works as a regular slider.

The rest of the interface changed with it. Your covers now light up blue when they're open, matching the sky behind the drawing, on tiles and card features as well. And the icons got a refresh: awnings finally have an icon of their own, gates open and close with proper arrows, and the window icon now looks like a window wherever you live.

This one started as an [Open Home Foundation roadmap opportunity](https://github.com/OpenHomeFoundation/roadmap/issues/33), and it took a whole group to get here. It went through several rounds of design, a clickable prototype, and lots of feedback from the community. The team at [MotionBlinds](https://works-with.home-assistant.io/certified-products/?brand=motionblinds), a [Works with Home Assistant](https://works-with.home-assistant.io/) partner, even shared the designs from their own app, showing every type of cover they make.

Thanks, [@mbb365], for the design, [@piitaya], for building it, and [@jlpouffier], [@matthiasdebaat], [@mkerstner], and [@abb848] for steering it through the roadmap! And thanks to [@wendevlin] for reviewing it, and everyone who tried the prototype and shared their thoughts. ❤️

## Try the next generation of voice

Your {% term "voice satellite" %} listens for its wake word on the device itself. That check has to be quick and has to run on a tiny chip, so it is a little eager. You probably know the result: the television says something that sounds a bit like "Okay Nabu", and suddenly your living room lights up. Not great during a movie.

The next generation of voice fixes this with a second opinion. When a satellite wakes up, Home Assistant sends that bit of audio to a much larger and smarter wake word model, which double-checks whether you really said the wake word. If you didn't, nothing happens. That larger model runs in a new Voice Support {% term app %}, on your own hardware.

That second opinion opens the door to two more improvements:

- **No more waiting for the beep.** Say the wake word and your command in one go: "Okay Nabu, turn on the lights." With next-generation voice turned on, there is no wake sound to wait for.
- **Fewer cut-off commands.** The app also listens for when you stop talking. It is better at hearing quiet voices and children, so a short pause or a soft voice no longer ends your command halfway.

Want to try it? Labs is where you can try features that are still in development. Go to {% my labs title="**Settings** > **System** > **Labs**" %} and turn on **Next-generation voice**. On {% term "Home Assistant Operating System" %}, Home Assistant installs the app and sets up the new [Voice support](/integrations/voice_support/) integration for you. Changed your mind? Turn it off again, and the app is removed. If you run {% term "Home Assistant Container" %}, you run the app yourself; the [documentation](/integrations/voice_support/#running-the-app-in-a-container) explains how.

Before you turn it on:

- **It's a preview.** This is in Labs for a reason. It is still in development and may change, and we'd love to hear how it works in your home.
- **Supported wake words.** It currently verifies **Okay Nabu**, **Hey Jarvis**, and **Hey Mycroft**. If a satellite has another wake word active as well, it keeps working as it does today, without the second opinion.
- **Voice Preview Edition.** The [Home Assistant Voice Preview Edition](/voice-pe/) needs its beta firmware for this. The [documentation](/integrations/voice_support/#installing-the-beta-firmware-on-the-voice-preview-edition) shows how to install it.

Thanks, [@arturpragacz] and [@synesthesiam]!

### Your voice timers, now in Home Assistant

"Okay Nabu, set a timer for 10 minutes." Until now, you could only check or change that timer by voice.

ESPHome voice satellites, like the [Home Assistant Voice Preview Edition](/voice-pe/), now keep their timers in a new [timer list](/integrations/timer_list/) {% term entity %}. That makes them part of your home. Start a timer from an {% term automation %}, like a three-minute tea timer when the kettle switches off. Add time to it, pause it, or cancel it. Or send a {% term notification %} to your phone when it finishes, in case you are not in the kitchen to hear it.

And you don't need an automation to see them. The new timer list card shows all timers of a satellite with a live countdown. Start a new one, add time, pause, or cancel, right from your dashboard. Select the entity, and its dialog gives you the same controls. So you can check how long the pasta still needs, without asking.

## Buttons that do more

A button on the wall is one of the nicest ways to control your home. Everybody in the house understands it, no phone or voice command needed. But a button with only one trick is a bit of a waste. Most remotes and wall switches can tell the difference between a single press, a double press, and holding the button down. Using that in Home Assistant meant digging through device triggers that look different for every brand, or writing a {% term template %} to read the event type. Not exactly the kind of thing you set up in a minute.

This release lays the groundwork to fix that. Buttons get a set of triggers that work the same way, whatever brand the button is.

The [**Button pressed**](/triggers/button.pressed/) trigger isn't just for the buttons on your dashboard anymore. It now works with the buttons on your remotes and wall switches too. Under **Number of presses**, pick a double, triple, or quadruple press. One press turns on the ceiling light. Two presses turn on every light in the room. Three presses? That one's up to you. 😉

Holding a physical button gets its own triggers:

- [**Button held**](/triggers/button.held/) fires when you hold a button down. Set an **Interval**, and it keeps firing while you hold it. Hold the button to dim the light, step by step, until it is just right.
- [**Button hold ended**](/triggers/button.hold_ended/) fires when you let go. Start the blinds when you hold the button, and stop them when you release it.

And there is a new [**Button is being held**](/conditions/button.is_being_held/) {% term condition %}, which checks whether a button is held down right now. Use it like a shift key: a button does something else while you hold another one.

You set all of this up in the automation editor, just like any other {% term trigger %}. Pick your remote or switch as the target, or an entire {% term area %}, and select what should happen.

A few things to know:

- **This is the start.** The triggers work with buttons whose integration reports presses in the new, standard way, and integrations are moving over one by one. Right now, [Universal Devices ISY/IoX](/integrations/isy994/) supports all of it. [Lutron Caséta](/integrations/lutron_caseta/) and [OpenDisplay](/integrations/opendisplay/) support **Button pressed**. More will follow in the coming releases. Integration developers can find the details in the [developer blog](https://contributors.home-assistant.io/blog/2026/11/09/button-event-types-revised/).
- **Not every button can do everything.** A double press only works if the button reports multiple presses, and repeating while held only works if it reports both the start and the end of a hold. The documentation of each trigger explains the details.
- **Existing automations keep working.** Nothing changes until you use the new options.

Thanks, [@abmantis] and [@nielsrowinbik]!

## A camera player that works the same everywhere

Opening a camera in Home Assistant used to give you the video player of your browser. So it looked and worked differently in Chrome, Safari, Firefox, and the {% term "Companion apps" %}. And some things, like picture in picture for some types of streams, were just not there.

The camera dialog now has a player of its own, with the same controls everywhere:

- **Zoom in.** Pinch, double-click, or hold <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>Cmd</kbd> and scroll to zoom in on the details. Drag to look around. A small map shows which part of the image you are looking at.
- **Picture in picture.** Keep the camera in a small floating window, so you can keep an eye on the front door while you do something else. This now also works for MJPEG cameras.
- **See how it connects.** There are different ways a video stream can reach you, like WebRTC, HLS, or MJPEG. A badge shows which one your camera uses. Select it for an explanation of what that means.
- **The screen stays on** while the camera is open, so your tablet doesn't go to sleep while you watch who's at the door.

## A smarter Energy dashboard

### Batteries, measured instead of guessed

Until now, the [Energy dashboard](/docs/energy/) only knew how much energy went into your home battery and how much came out. Where that energy came from, and where it went, was an estimate. And estimates can be wrong: charge your battery from the grid at night, and the dashboard could show it as solar charging.

If your battery reports it, you can now measure it per direction: from the grid, from solar, to your home, and to the grid. When you add a battery, the first question is what your battery measures, and the form follows from there. The energy cards and the power flow chart then use the real numbers, and only estimate what isn't measured. Batteries that only report the total going in and out keep working exactly as before.

And if your battery reports its usable capacity, you can now pick that sensor for the battery capacity, instead of typing in a number. Useful for batteries whose capacity changes over time.

The energy distribution card shows the flows between solar, grid, battery, and home.' class='no-shadow' />

Thanks, [@MindFreeze] and [@thomasvt1]!

### Energy meters you forgot about

Who hasn't added a new smart plug and forgotten to add it to the Energy dashboard? Or never noticed that the inverter integration also gives you a solar energy sensor?

{% my config_energy title="**Settings** > **Energy**" %} now tells you. It lists the energy, gas, and water meters that keep track of your usage, but aren't in your Energy dashboard yet, on the tab they belong to. Pick where each one belongs, like grid consumption, solar production, or an individual device, and select **Add**. Not interested? Select **Dismiss**, and it won't bother you again.

### More energy improvements

- **Energy settings on a page of their own.** Sources and devices in the energy settings now open on a full page, instead of a dialog. Thanks, [@MindFreeze]!
- **One line per type.** Have several solar inverters or grid connections? The energy cards can now combine sources of the same type into one entry, using **Combine sources** in the date picker. The Energy dashboard does this on the Electricity, Gas, and Water tabs. Thanks, [@MindFreeze]!
- **Gas devices.** Just like water, you can now add individual gas devices, like your stove or boiler, to the Energy dashboard. Thanks, [@Jump-333]!
- **What did it cost?** The electricity usage graph shows the net cost of the selected period, including what you got back for the energy you returned to the grid. Thanks, [@kdvlr]!
- **Small things.** The period selector shows the day of the week when you look at a single day. When comparing, the Totals table shows the previous values below the current ones, so it no longer needs a scrollbar on narrow cards. And **Power sources (graph)** is now called **Power profile (graph)**, which better describes what it shows. Thanks, [@TCWORLD], [@sadilek], and [@NoRi2909]!

## Integrations

### New integrations

We welcome the following new integrations in this release:

- **[Bambu Lab]**, added by [@joostlek]  
  Follow your Bambu Lab 3D printer over your local network, without the Bambu Lab cloud. See the print status and progress, the current layer, when the print finishes, and the bed and chamber temperature. Get a {% term notification %} when your print is done, without walking to the printer.

- **[BenQ Projector Infrared]**, added by [@rrooggiieerr], launching at 🥈 silver quality  
  Control a BenQ projector using an infrared emitter that is already set up in Home Assistant. Add an infrared receiver, and Home Assistant even follows along when you use the projector's own remote. Movie night, one tap.

- **[BUSY Bar]**, added by [@truetug]  
  Keep an eye on the battery and Wi-Fi connection of your [BUSY Bar](https://busy.app), a desktop status display, over your local network.

- **[Croma Infrared]**, added by [@Dr-Blank]  
  Control a Croma Google TV, sold in India by Croma, using an infrared emitter that is already set up in Home Assistant. Turn the TV off at bedtime, or open YouTube, Netflix, or Prime Video as part of a {% term scene %}, without the TV on your network. Infrared is one-way, so the integration cannot read the TV's state.

- **[Daikin Infrared]**, added by [@javitonino], launching at 🥈 silver quality  
  Control a Daikin air conditioner using an infrared emitter that is already set up in Home Assistant. With an infrared receiver, it also follows the Daikin remote, so Home Assistant knows when someone changes the temperature the old-fashioned way. Is your unit on your network? Then the [Daikin AC](/integrations/daikin/) integration can read its real state.

- **[E3/DC]**, added by [@AlCalzone], launching at 🥈 silver quality  
  Connect an E3/DC home power station from HagerEnergy over your local network. See the power flowing between your solar panels, battery, house, grid, and wallboxes, and know when your house runs on backup power.

- **[Electrolux]**, added by [@electrolux-oss]  
  Connect your Electrolux, AEG, and Frigidaire appliances, with an integration built by Electrolux itself. It supports a long list of appliances: dishwashers, washing machines, dryers, fridges, ovens, hobs, hoods, air purifiers, air conditioners, dehumidifiers, and robot vacuums. Get a {% term notification %} when the laundry is done, or start the vacuum when everyone leaves the house.

- **[Fujitsu Infrared]**, added by [@Dr-Blank], launching at 🥈 silver quality  
  Control a Fujitsu General air conditioner, also sold as O General, using an infrared emitter that is already set up in Home Assistant. With an infrared receiver, it follows the physical remote too.

- **[Intesis Modbus]**, added by [@TimoPtr]  
  Control an air conditioner connected to an [Intesis](https://www.intesis.com) Modbus gateway, locally over a serial connection. Turn it on and off, and change its mode, target temperature, and fan speed. This first version supports the gateway for a single Mitsubishi Heavy Industries indoor unit.

- **[NAD Infrared]**, added by [@rrooggiieerr], launching at 🥈 silver quality  
  Control NAD receivers, amplifiers, CD players, and cassette decks using an infrared emitter that is already set up in Home Assistant. For example, turn on your receiver and switch to the right input when your TV turns on.

- **[Powersensor]**, added by [@bookman-dius]  
  Bring your [Powersensor](https://www.powersensor.com.au) plugs and sensors into Home Assistant, locally. Measure the power and energy of your mains, solar, or a single appliance, or the water use of your home.

- **[Sony Infrared]**, added by [@googanhiem]

- **[Timer list]**, added by [@synesthesiam]

- **[Trimlight]**, added by [@bsholy]  
  Control [Trimlight](https://trimlight.com/) Edge Pro permanent outdoor lighting over your local network. For example, turn off your outdoor lights at bedtime.

### Noteworthy improvements to existing integrations

It is not just new {% term integrations %} that have been added; existing ones keep getting better too. Here are some of the noteworthy improvements this release:

- **[Matter]** now supports the new closure devices from Matter 1.5, like garage doors, gates, and roof windows, as {% term covers %}. Matter cameras that support it, like the Aqara Camera Hub G350, can switch night vision on, off, or to auto. And battery-powered devices tell you how many batteries they need, in addition to the battery type. Thanks, [@lboue] and [@tomfrenzel]!
- **[Tuya]** had a big month, mostly thanks to new contributors. Air conditioners gained Eco and Sleep switches, presence sensors with fall detection report a fall, and smart circuit breakers report their leakage current, temperature, and faults. Pet feeders with a camera report the food level and when food gets stuck, and TION Breezer fresh air ventilators, single-phase power clamps, EV chargers, and a new type of security camera are now supported too. Standing desks show their height. Thanks, [@alberttwong], [@nobeautifulsalt], [@XiyeNishino], [@coen1111], [@untcoder2], [@saihtaM], [@kupka], [@jjprz], and [@mhalano]!
- **[ESPHome]** devices can now receive radio frequency signals as well, so a handheld remote or a 433 MHz sensor can reach Home Assistant through an ESPHome device. Thanks, [@abmantis]!
- **[Shelly]** fans are now real fan {% term entities %}, with a boost switch and the sensors of the fan. Plugs and power strips gained a setting for what their LED shows, and Gen2+ switches and covers can lock their physical input, so little fingers can't turn things on. Switches report how long and how often they have been on, the Wall Display gained brightness controls, and the new `shelly.command` {% term action %} lets you send any command to a device, for features without an entity. Thanks, [@thecode], [@bieniu], [@YuvalWS], and [@chemelli74]!
- **[Philips Hue]** gradient lights, like the Play gradient lightstrip, Signe, and Festavia, can now set the color of each segment separately. The segments are hidden by default, so the light keeps working as one light, until you need more. Thanks, [@marcelveldt]!
- **[HomeKit Bridge]** cameras can now record to HomeKit Secure Video. Turn it on per camera in the options, and link a motion sensor to it. Thanks, [@RaHehl]!
- **[Music Assistant]** gained a sleep timer, crossfade and autoplay switches, the settings of your Sonos speakers, like loudness, night mode, and speech enhancement, and a shuffle option when you start playing an album or playlist. Thanks, [@joostlek], [@fmunkes], and [@marcelveldt]!
- **[Ring]** cameras can talk back: they now work with the two-way audio preview in Labs. Thanks, [@cmatte]!
- **[Reolink]** recordings now have thumbnails in the media browser, instead of placeholders. Thanks, [@michaljonik]!
- **[UniFi Network]** shows the schedule of your firewall policies, and can change it. Block the kids' network until tomorrow morning, right from an {% term automation %}. Gateways also get an {% term update "update entity" %} for their IPS engine. Thanks, [@bhamiltoncx] and [@IvanAlekseev]!
- **[UniFi Protect]** supports smoke detectors, with events for smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, and for maintenance, like a low battery or a self-test. Thanks, [@RaHehl]!
- **[Alexa Devices]** now supports the Amazon Smart Thermostat and the Amazon Smart Plug. Thanks, [@chemelli74] and [@jamesonuk]!
- **[SwitchBot Bluetooth]** can sign in with your SwitchBot account during setup, to find your devices and fetch the encryption key of encrypted devices, like locks, without typing it in yourself. Thanks, [@zerzhang]!
- **[HomeWizard]** Energy Sockets can now use the local v2 API, so you can turn them on and off and use the switch lock. Thanks, [@lexpostma] and [@nvanofwegen]!
- **[FRITZ!Box Tools]** shows the signal strength and the link speed of each connected device. **[Synology DSM]** shows an update entity for each package on your NAS. Thanks, [@mib1185] and [@epenet]!
- **[ONVIF]** cameras report events like line crossing as an {% term "event entity" %}. Thanks, [@jterrace]!
- **[VeSync]** air fryers can be paused, resumed, and stopped, tell you when the cook is done, and dual-basket fryers are supported. Humidifiers got their RGB night light as a light, purifiers report their filter life and VOC level, and fans their temperature. Thanks, [@cdnninja], [@KindOfNerdy], [@FredM67], [@MangelSpec], and [@joshje]!
- **[KNX]** can now suggest entities based on your ETS project. It knows what each channel is, like a light switching actuator, so you don't have to set up every group address by hand. And its AI tools can now create those entities for you. Thanks, [@farmio] and [@ab-tools]!
- **[Overkiz]** ovens can be started and stopped, and report their door, whether they reached the temperature, and when they're done, and Atlantic towel dryers that are controlled with heating levels now work. Thanks, [@Mincka] and [@Aarklendoia]!
- **[HomematicIP Cloud]** added the universal dimmer as a light, an {% term action %} to arm the alarm even when a window is still open, the valve positions of floor heating actuators, and color temperature for the light strip controller. Thanks, [@lackas]!
- **[Viessmann ViCare]** heating circuits gained an operating mode select, heat pumps report their heat production, and Vitoset Aqua water softeners report their salt supply and water use. Rooms report their temperature, humidity, and condensation risk. Thanks, [@lackas], [@tomekwl], and [@skornehl]!
- **[Freebox]** can now control roller shutters paired with a Freebox Delta or Freebox Home, and shows the traffic of its LAN ports. Thanks, [@slater0013] and [@davinov]!
- **[Škoda]** grew up fast, with a whole group of new contributors. See where your car is parked, whether its doors, windows, and trunk are closed, start and stop charging and the active ventilation, set the charge limit, and turn on the air conditioning to the temperature you want. Thanks, [@pballhausen], [@DavidMagyarics], and [@dvx76]!
- **[System Bridge]** turns your computer into a proper device in Home Assistant: control the brightness and power of its displays, put it to sleep, restart, or lock it, run your saved commands, and react when you select a notification on it. The media player can now seek, set the volume, and show the cover art. Thanks, [@timmo001] and [@eikw]!
- **[Bosch SHC]** keeps growing: events for each key of the Universal Switch, alarm and tamper sensors for the Outdoor Siren, and more for the Motion Detector II. **[Bosch Smart Camera]** gained a privacy mode switch, the camera light, and a firmware update entity. Thanks, [@mosandlt]!
- **[SleepIQ]** shows when you went to bed and got up, how long you slept restfully and how long it took you to fall asleep, lets you set your favorite firmness, and turns Responsive Air on and off. Thanks, [@veteranbv]!
- **[Withings]** scales report your metabolic age, and the sleep sensors gained the time in bed, sleep efficiency, and, where available, the apnea-hypopnea index. **[Google Health]** added sleep, nutrition, heart rate variability, and oxygen saturation sensors. Thanks, [@SH1FT-W], [@timboothby], and [@allenporter]!
- **[Tractive]** reports the health status of your pet's resting heart rate, breathing, and scratching, and **[Fressnapf Tracker]** tells you when the tracker last checked in. Thanks, [@f4llenz] and [@Uli112Winsen]!
- **[Zonneplan]** shows the electricity price per quarter hour, and an {% term action %} returns the upcoming prices, so you can charge your car in the cheapest hours. Thanks, [@bartvanraaij]!
- For the weather fans: **[Foreca]** gained sensors and forecast actions, including air quality, **[IRM KMI Weather Belgium]** added weather warnings, pollen, and a rain radar, and **[SMHI]** and **[Stookwijzer]** now show their radar and advice map as an image. Thanks, [@EetuPelkonen], [@YP30], [@silamon], [@gjohansson-ST], and [@fwestenberg]!
- **[Home Assistant Supervisor]** has a disk lifetime sensor, so you can get a warning before your SSD wears out. Thanks, [@MB901]!
- **[LiteLLM]** can now be used for {% term TTS "text-to-speech" %} and AI tasks, and **[Google Cloud]** text-to-speech starts speaking sooner with Chirp 3 HD voices. Thanks, [@lucemans] and [@mikahtr]!

### What is Home Assistant doing?

Home Assistant does a lot behind the scenes. Most of the time, that's exactly how it should be: things just work, and you don't have to think about it. But when something is slow, broken, or quietly eating your disk space, you want answers. And until now, those answers were often hidden in a log file.

This release gives you a better look under the hood, without opening a single log file.

#### Watch Home Assistant start

Restarting Home Assistant used to be a bit of a waiting game. A message told you it was still starting, and named the integration that was taking the longest. That made it look like Home Assistant was working through your {% term integrations %} one at a time, while in reality, it sets up many of them at once.

Now, while Home Assistant starts, select the info button on the startup message to see what is going on, live. How many integrations are set up, which ones are setting up right now and for how long, and which ones failed. Once Home Assistant is up and running, it tells you if any integration needs your attention. The details show how long the startup took and which integrations were the slowest. So the next time a restart takes forever, you know who to blame. 😉

<img src='/images/blog/2026-12/startup-details.png' alt='Screenshot of the startup details dialog while Home Assistant starts. It shows 122 of 160 integrations set up after 29 seconds, with a progress bar, and a list of the integrations that are setting up right now, like Sonos, Z-Wave, and Synology DSM, each with how long it has been setting up.' class='no-shadow' />

#### Is this integration healthy?

An integration that shows as loaded isn't always fine. A sensor might be unavailable, there might be an open repair, or the log might be filling up with errors. Each of those lives in a different place in Home Assistant.

Each integration entry now has a **Health** item in its three-dots menu, which brings it all together. It tells you whether the entry is running and, if not, why. It shows how many of its {% term entities %} are available, the open repairs, the recent errors and warnings in the log, and how long it took to set up. Each problem links straight to the place where you can fix it.

<img src='/images/blog/2026-12/integration-health.png' alt='Screenshot of the health dialog of a printer, saying that some things need a look. The setup failed because Home Assistant could not connect to the printer, and will be retried. None of its 5 entities, like the ink levels, are available, with a link to view them. There are no open repairs, and no errors or warnings in the recent log, and the integration took 3.1 seconds to start.' class='no-shadow' />

#### Why is my database so big?

That question comes up a lot, and until now, the answer was a single number. Unless you were up for writing your own database queries, that is.

The **Storage** page in {% my storage title="**Settings** > **System** > **Storage**" %} now has a **Database** card. Select **Calculate**, give it a moment on a big database, and it shows how much space your database uses, which tables take up that space, and how much of it is unused. And, most importantly, which {% term entities %} are responsible. Sort and search the list. Spotted a sensor that is writing a new value every second? Select it to open its settings, and decide whether you need to keep all that history. Made some changes? Select **Recalculate** to measure again.

The **Storage** page used to be available only on {% term "Home Assistant Operating System" %} and supervised installations. It now shows up on every installation type, so {% term "Home Assistant Container" %} users get the **Database** card too.

### Your AI assistant can do more

Last release, your AI assistant learned to look into Home Assistant and help you troubleshoot. This release, it learns to help you set things up and keep them tidy. Paulus and Allen went on quite a tool-building spree this month. 😅

Say you just unpacked a box of new sensors, and Home Assistant discovered all of them. Normally, that's the start of an evening of setting them up one by one, renaming them, and putting each one in the right room. Now you can ask: "Add the new sensors, name them after the room they're in, put them in that area, and give them a Security label." Your assistant adds the integration, renames the devices, and sorts them into your areas. You check the result.

Or: "Why didn't the hallway light turn on last night?" Your assistant reads the automation, looks at what it did last night, and tells you. Or: "Is my home battery set up right in the Energy dashboard?"

That assistant doesn't have to live inside Home Assistant. Your favorite AI app or agent, like Claude, ChatGPT, or Cursor, can do the same through the [Model Context Protocol](/integrations/mcp_server/) server. Model Context Protocol, or MCP, is a standard way for AI apps to connect to other apps, like Home Assistant. Turn it on in a single click under **Settings** > **System** > **AI**, paste the URL into your AI app, and it can help you set up your home, using the same tools.

Let's start with the everyday stuff, which works for everyone in your home:

- **"Turn off the lights in 5 minutes"** now works from any device, not only from voice satellites that support timers.
- **Broadcast to a room.** "Tell the kids dinner is ready" can now go to the speakers in one {% term area %} or on one floor, instead of every speaker in the house.

What an assistant can do depends on the [AI tools](/integrations/llm/) you give it. They come in sets: **Device control** lets it control the devices you exposed to voice assistants, and the **Home Assistant** set lets it manage Home Assistant itself. That second set is for administrators only, and it grows a lot:

- **Add integrations and helpers.** Your assistant can see which discovered devices are waiting for you, add an {% term integration %} or helper, and change the options of an integration you already have.
- **Organize your home.** Create and change {% term areas %}, {% term floors %}, and {% term labels %}. Rename {% term devices %} and {% term entities %}, put them in the right area, hide or disable them, and set the unit and display precision of a sensor. It can also read and change your home's information, like its location, time zone, and unit system.
- **Understand your automations.** It can read your {% term automations %}, {% term scripts %}, {% term scenes %}, and {% term blueprints %}, and the traces of what they did.
- **Energy and history.** It can read and change your Energy dashboard settings, and search your {% term "long-term statistics" %}, even by the name you use for a sensor.
- **Repairs and apps.** It can list your open repairs, and your installed apps, including which ones have an update.

Two things to keep in mind:

- **These tools change things, without asking first.** That's the point, but it's also why you choose when to hand them out. Add the **Home Assistant** set only to an assistant you use to manage Home Assistant, and keep a separate one with **Device control** for everyday use. The [AI tools documentation](/integrations/llm/#setting-up-a-separate-assistant-for-the-home-assistant-ai-tools) shows how.
- **You stay in charge.** Only administrators can use these tools, and you decide which assistant or AI app gets them. Want an AI app that can only look, not touch? Set the Model Context Protocol server to expose read-only tools.

## Backward-incompatible changes

We do our best to avoid making changes to existing functionality that might unexpectedly impact your Home Assistant installation. Unfortunately, sometimes it is inevitable.

We always make sure to document these changes to make the transition as easy as possible for you. This release has the following backward-incompatible changes:

{% details "AccuWeather" %}

The values of two AccuWeather sensor attributes have changed so they can be translated:

- The `direction` attribute of the forecast `Wind speed day`, `Wind speed night`, `Wind gust speed day` and `Wind gust speed night` sensors is now lowercase, for example `nw` instead of `NW`.
- The `level` attribute of the `UV index` sensor is now `very_high` instead of `very high`. The other levels (`low`, `moderate`, `high`, `extreme`) are unchanged.

If you use these attribute values in automations, scripts or templates, update them to the new values.

{% details "AprilAire" %}

The AprilAire integration now identifies a thermostat by its MAC address in the standard zero-padded form (for example `b4:82:55:00:1a:05`). Earlier versions dropped the leading zero of some parts of the address (`b4:82:55:0:1a:5`).

{% details "Matter" %}

Matter devices that expose the same kind of outlet on several endpoints, such as the sockets of a multi-socket smart plug, are now represented as separate child devices when the device names them, with semantic tags or a known label.

Previously all non-bridged endpoints of a node were collapsed onto a single device and the duplicated entities were disambiguated with a numeric or label suffix, for example `Switch (top)` and `Switch (bottom)`. Each such outlet now becomes its own child device of the main device, named for example `Top`, `Bottom` or `Channel 1`, and its entities drop the suffix.

Existing entities keep their entity IDs and keep working in automations, scripts and dashboards, and targeting the main device still controls all of its child devices. The affected entities do move onto new child devices though, which is semantically a breaking change, so device based grouping or per entity customizations on those entities may need to be reviewed after the update.

The `friendly_name` of the affected entities changes, because the displayed name is built from the entity's own device and its entity name. On the Eve Energy dual outlet, `switch.eve_energy_20ecn4101_switch_top` goes from `Eve Energy 20ECN4101 Switch (top)` to `Top`. Templates and notifications that use `friendly_name` are affected. If you renamed one of these entities, the child device name replaces the main device's name, for example a renamed `Kettle` on the bottom outlet goes from `Eve Energy 20ECN4101 Kettle` to `Bottom Kettle`.

Only On/Off Plug-in Unit endpoints (outlets and relays) that appear on more than one endpoint of the same device, and that the device names, are split. When the device reports per-outlet power and energy through a separate measurement endpoint linked by Power Topology, those sensors move to the outlet's child device too and lose their numeric suffix. Multi-button remotes, bridged devices and composed appliances are unchanged, apart from the parts of a split outlet, which go to its child device.

([@MartinHjelmare] - [#186033]) ([Matter documentation])

[#186033]: https://github.com/home-assistant/core/pull/186033

{% details "Recorder and SQL" %}

SQLAlchemy, the database library behind the recorder and the SQL integration, now URL-decodes the database name or SQLite file path in a database URL. {% details "Zigbee Home Automation" %}
