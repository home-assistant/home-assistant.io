Home Assistant 2026.10! 🎉

Last week, Nabu Casa dropped: [Big Tech ruined the cloud, so we're renaming ours](/blog/2026/10/02/big-tech-ruined-the-cloud-so-were-renaming-ours/). Home Assistant Cloud is on its way to becoming Home Assistant Link. Same service, new name. I love that title, by the way. If you haven't read the story behind it yet, go have a look, and [tell us what you think](#cloud-becomes-link). The change is planned for the December release, so there is still time. 🏎️

Now, I'll let you in on a secret: I'm an automation person. Dashboards are lovely and I admire everyone who builds a pretty one, but for me, {% term automations %} are what make a smart home actually smart. So you can imagine how happy I am with what's in this release. One of the most powerful features you never thought you'd need has been sitting in Home Assistant for years: trigger IDs. Give your {% term triggers %} an ID, and one automation can do the job of two, three, or five. It was also tucked away in a menu, you had to invent the IDs yourself, and then remember which was which. Plenty of you never found it, and that's on us.

Not anymore. [Trigger IDs finally get a proper UI](#the-best-automation-feature-you-never-used): you check the triggers you want in the condition, and Home Assistant takes care of the IDs behind the scenes. And if you like writing your own IDs in {% term YAML %}, nothing changes. Those keep working exactly as before. That makes it my favorite of this release. 🧠

Don't get me wrong, the map is absolutely a cool feature too. Last release, every map in Home Assistant broke overnight when our map provider wanted an API key. We moved to [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/), and it turns out that swapping out the map opens a lot of doors: [a vector map that stays sharp at every zoom level](#a-whole-new-map), redesigned markers, and an overview of where everyone has been. You are here. 📍

There's more: a [tidier profile page](#a-tidier-profile-page), a [panel for your Modbus connections](#a-panel-for-your-modbus-connections), [connecting your AI in one click](#connect-your-ai-in-one-click), and 15 new {% term integrations %}! 🚀

Enjoy the release!

## Cloud becomes Link

In case you missed it, [we're making the move](/blog/2026/10/02/big-tech-ruined-the-cloud-so-were-renaming-ours/) to rename [Home Assistant Cloud](/cloud/) to Home Assistant Link. Same service, new name.

We're all pretty annoyed by how Big Tech has been ruining perfectly good smart home devices by making them reliant on the cloud. Ours has never locked anyone in, harvested their data, or turned working devices into e-waste, so a new name was in order. Home Assistant Link is all about being clearer that it does things differently. The change is planned for the December release, so tell us your thoughts before then.

The best link is a fast link. 🏎️ [Nabu Casa](https://www.nabucasa.com/) has been hard at work making remote access even faster across the globe. They recently [migrated all of their servers to bare metal](https://support.nabucasa.com/hc/en-us/articles/39385482184733) and added more servers to boost their coverage of the world. This month, they added a latency checker that pings multiple servers on boot and picks the best connection for you.

## A whole new map

Last release, something changed underneath every map in Home Assistant, and we barely mentioned it in the release notes. The map provider we relied on started stamping an "API key required" message across its map images, breaking every map in Home Assistant at once. Instead of hunting for another free tile provider, we switched from pre-rendered map images to vector tiles generated and served by the OpenStreetMap Foundation. Both maps use [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/) data, but your device now draws the map itself. The new [Map tiles](/integrations/map_tiles/) integration fetches and caches the tiles through your Home Assistant instance, so your browser does not contact external tile servers directly. Recently viewed parts of the map remain available through a short internet hiccup.

Guillaume Rischard ([@grischard]), the former chair of the OpenStreetMap Foundation who initiated and led its vector-tile initiative, joined us during the [2026.9 release party](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TgGiUchnmDw).

Swapping out the map underneath opened a lot of doors. This release walks through the first few.

[@grischard]: https://github.com/grischard

### Crisp at every zoom level

The map is drawn entirely on your device, as vectors, instead of being stitched together from pictures. Streets and labels stay sharp at any zoom level, and zooming in and out is smooth. The map also has a dedicated dark style, with colors designed for dark mode. Older devices that can't render vector maps, like the old tablet on your wall, automatically fall back to the classic map.

### Redesigned markers

The markers on the map got a complete redesign. People show up with their picture, {% term zones %} get a marker with their icon in their own color, and everyone in the same zone shares a single bubble on that zone, instead of a pile of markers stacked on top of each other. Zoom out far enough, and bubbles that would overlap merge into one.

<img src='/images/blog/2026-10/map-markers.png' alt='Screenshot of the map with the redesigned markers, showing a person with their picture, two people sharing a bubble on the Home zone, and the Supermarket zone with its icon, next to the overview listing everyone on the People tab.' class='no-shadow' />

### An overview for the Map dashboard

The [Map dashboard](/dashboards/dashboards/#map-dashboard) now comes with an overview, next to the map on a desktop and as a sheet you pull up on your phone. It has three tabs: **People**, **Devices**, and **Zones**. Select someone to see their recent activity, with each stop colored by the zone they were in. Select a zone to see who's there. On a phone, the map now also fills the whole screen, edge to edge.

<img src='/images/blog/2026-10/map-activity.png' alt='Screenshot of the recent activity of a person in the map overview, with each entry marked in the color of its zone.' class='no-shadow' />

The same overview shows up when you put a [map card](/dashboards/map/) in a panel view on your own dashboard.

### Zones on the new map

The zone editor in {% my zones title="**Settings** > **Areas, labels & zones**" %} now uses the same new map. Your zones are sorted alphabetically, with Home always at the top, and they show the same icons and colors you see on the map.

<img src='/images/blog/2026-10/map-zone-editor.png' alt='Screenshot of the zone editor on the new map, with the list of zones sorted alphabetically and Home at the top.' class='no-shadow' />

The redesign follows an [Open Home Foundation design concept](https://github.com/OpenHomeFoundation/ux-design/discussions/68) for the Map dashboard.

## A tidier profile page

Select your name at the bottom of the sidebar, and you land on your {% my profile title="**User profile**" %}. Over the years, that page quietly turned into a bit of a junk drawer. Language, theme, date formats, sidebar order, push notifications, keyboard shortcuts: all stacked in one long **General** tab, with **Security** as its only neighbor. Everything was there. You just had to scroll past most of it to find the one thing you came for.

The profile page is now an overview, using the same kind of navigation you already know from **Settings**. At the top is a card with your account, followed by your **Theme**, and a short list of sections that each hold a handful of related settings:

- **Appearance**: Your default dashboard and the order of your sidebar
- **Localization**: Your language, time zone, and how dates, times, and numbers are shown
- **This browser** (or **This mobile app** in the companion app): Notifications, display, and shortcuts for the device you're using right now
- **Security**: Your password, [multi-factor authentication](/docs/authentication/multi-factor-auth/), and access tokens

Nothing was taken away. Every setting is still there, just grouped so it's easier to find. Thanks, [@pcan08] and [@maartenla]!

<img src='/images/blog/2026-10/profile-page.png' alt='Screenshot of the redesigned user profile page, showing the account card, the theme picker, and the Appearance, Localization, and This browser sections.' class='no-shadow' />

That account card at the top learned a new trick, too. Until now, your own name and picture weren't actually yours to change. Only an administrator could set them, tucked away in the person settings. Not an administrator, but you wanted a nicer picture of yourself in the sidebar? You had to go ask whoever runs your Home Assistant. A bit odd, for something that is literally your face.

Every user, administrator or not, now has an **Edit profile** button right next to **Log out**. Upload and crop a picture, change your name, and save. Your name is used for both your user and the [person](/integrations/person/) linked to it, so it's the same everywhere you show up, from the sidebar to your dashboards and [Assist](/voice_control/). Your new picture shows up in the sidebar right away.

## The best automation feature you never used

Take a look at your {% term automations %}, and chances are you'll find a few that come in pairs. One turns the hallway light on when motion is detected, the other turns it off again when the motion stops. One sends a notification when the washing machine starts, the other when it's done. Two automations, doing one job.

Home Assistant has had a way to combine those into one for years. Give each {% term trigger %} an [ID](/docs/automation/trigger/#trigger-id), and use the [**Triggered by**](/conditions/trigger/) {% term condition %} to do something different depending on which trigger fired. It's one of the most powerful things you can do in the [automation editor](/docs/automation/editor/), and also one of the best-hidden. The trigger ID field sat in the menu of each trigger. You had to come up with an ID, type it in yourself, and then remember which ID belonged to which trigger when you picked them in the condition. Most people never found it, and honestly, we can't blame them.

Not anymore. Add a **Triggered by** condition, and you check the triggers it should match. You see the triggers themselves, not some ID you made up, and each one is numbered by its position in your list, with the same number shown next to the trigger itself. So you always know which one is which. There are no IDs to come up with at all. Home Assistant takes care of those behind the scenes, and cleans up after itself when you remove a trigger.

<img src='/images/blog/2026-10/automation-triggered-by.png' alt='Screenshot of the automation editor with two numbered triggers, Motion detected and Motion cleared, and a Triggered by condition where the first trigger, Motion detected, is checked.' class='no-shadow' />

If you set your trigger IDs yourself in {% term YAML %}, nothing changes. Those IDs keep working exactly as they did, sharing one ID between several triggers is still fine, and Home Assistant won't add IDs to your YAML for you. The trigger ID field is gone from the automation editor, because you no longer need it there: to run the same actions for several triggers, check them all in one **Triggered by** condition.

The editor does keep an eye out for two things, but only in the **Triggered by** condition. If a condition points to a trigger that no longer exists, it flags it, so you can clear it. And if several triggers share the same ID, it shows a warning with a **Fix** that gives each trigger its own ID and updates your conditions to match. It's a suggestion, not a repair, so if sharing an ID is what you want, you can leave it as it is.

This completes [an Open Home Foundation roadmap opportunity](https://github.com/OpenHomeFoundation/roadmap/issues/66) to make referencing triggers in automations a lot easier. Thanks, [@jpbede], [@wendevlin], [@marcinbauer-ohf], and [@nielsrowinbik]!

While you're in the automation editor, you'll notice your {% term triggers %}, {% term conditions %}, and {% term actions %} tell you a bit more at a glance. Each row now shows the details that used to be hidden until you opened it: the threshold a trigger or condition checks for, like "above 10 °C", how long something needs to stay that way, the offset on a sunrise or sunset, and how long a wait action waits before it gives up. Thanks, [@wendevlin] and [@MindFreeze]!

### New integrations

We welcome the following new integrations in this release:

- **[Axle Energy]**, added by [@Herbertmt978]  
  Bring the grid event schedule of your [Axle Energy](https://www.axle.energy/) account into Home Assistant. It tells you whether an upcoming event asks you to import from or export to the grid, and exactly when it starts and ends, so your {% term automations %} can get your home ready in time.

- **[Bitvis Power Hub]**, added by [@MandusBorjesson]  
  Read real-time electricity data from your smart meter using a [Bitvis Power Hub](https://bitvis.se/), which plugs into the meter's <abbr title="Home Area Network">HAN</abbr> port. The Power Hub pushes its measurements, like power, voltage, and current per phase, straight to Home Assistant over your local network. No cloud, no {% term polling %}.

- **[Gree Infrared]**, added by [@Dr-Blank], launching at 🥈 silver quality  
  Control a Gree air conditioner using any infrared transmitter you already have set up in Home Assistant, like an ESPHome device with an infrared LED. Add an infrared receiver, and the {% term climate %} entity follows along when someone uses the physical Gree remote. Gree builds air conditioners for many brands, so units sold as Onida, Sinclair, and others work as well.

- **[INDI Allsky]**, added by [@TN-1]  
  Connect your [INDI Allsky](https://github.com/aaronwmorris/indi-allsky) camera server, an allsky camera system for astrophotography and keeping an eye on cloud cover. You get the latest capture as a camera, plus sensors for sky quality, the number of stars detected, exposure time, and whether it's night.

- **[KACO Modbus]**, added by [@g4bri3lDev]  
  Connect a [KACO new energy](https://kaco-newenergy.com/) solar inverter directly over your local network using Modbus TCP. It shows what the inverter is producing right now, its lifetime energy counter for the [Energy dashboard](/docs/energy/), and what it is doing, without any cloud account or manufacturer portal involved.

- **[my-PV]**, added by [@rrooggiieerr]  
  Control [my-PV](https://www.my-pv.com/) devices, like the AC•THOR range and AC ELWA 2, which turn your surplus solar power into hot water. Home Assistant talks to them locally and discovers them on your network automatically.

- **[OSRAM Infrared]**, added by [@freeDom-], launching at 🥈 silver quality  
  Control OSRAM and LEDVANCE RGB lights that come with a 24-button infrared remote, using an infrared transmitter set up in Home Assistant. With an infrared receiver, the {% term light %} entity also picks up commands from the physical remote.

- **[Persang Infrared]**, added by [@Dr-Blank]  
  Control a Persang Bluetooth speaker using an infrared transmitter set up in Home Assistant, giving you a media player and buttons for its remote functions.

- **[SolarEdge Modbus]**, added by [@frenck], launching at 🏆 platinum quality  
  Connect to your SolarEdge solar inverter over your own network, using the Modbus TCP interface built into the inverter. No account, no API key, no cloud. Readings arrive every few seconds instead of every few minutes, and keep arriving when your internet connection is down. You can also change the export limit and how your battery charges.

- **[Sunsynk]**, added by [@jamesridgway]  
  Bring your [Sunsynk](https://www.sunsynk.org) hybrid solar inverter and battery into Home Assistant using your Sunsynk Connect account. You get sensors for solar production, grid import and export, and your battery. Home Assistant can even discover the Sunsynk data logger on your network.

- **[Theben Conexa Smart Meter Gateway]**, added by [@mdluhosch]  
  Read your meter data locally from a [Theben Conexa 3.0](https://www.theben-se.de/conexa/) smart meter gateway, using its built-in Home Area Network (HAN) interface. No cloud needed.

- **[Threema]**, added by [@LukasQ]  
  Send end-to-end encrypted messages from your {% term automations %} to people on [Threema](https://threema.ch), the privacy-focused messenger that doesn't need a phone number. It uses the Threema Gateway service, and each person you add as a recipient gets their own notify entity.

- **[Vitesy]**, added by [@chemelli74]  
  Connect the [Vitesy](https://vitesy.com/) Shelfy, an air purifier that lives in your refrigerator. See the air quality and temperature inside your fridge, how often the door was opened, and when the filter needs cleaning or replacing. You can even switch the purifier mode, for example to boost it after a grocery run.

- **[Willow]**, added by [@PaxPrz]  
  Keep an eye on the health of your indoor plants with Willow sensors. Temperature, humidity, soil moisture, and light levels come from the Willow cloud into Home Assistant, ready for your dashboards and {% term automations %}. Your plants can't tell you they're thirsty, but now Home Assistant can.

- **[Zonneplan]**, added by [@erwindouna]  
  Get the electricity and gas prices of your [Zonneplan](https://www.zonneplan.nl/) contract, a Dutch provider of dynamic energy contracts. Handy for running your appliances when energy is cheapest.

### Noteworthy improvements to existing integrations

It is not just new {% term integrations %} that have been added; existing ones keep getting better too. Here are some of the noteworthy improvements this release:

- **[Matter]** added {% term switches %} to turn the visual and audible alarms of supported leak sensors on or off, like the IKEA KLIPPBOK, and a switch for one-touch locking, so you decide whether a single touch on the keypad of your lock is enough to lock the door. {% term Devices %} that measure electrical power now also report the frequency and power factor. Thanks, [@wardmatter], [@mmmspatz], and [@lboue]!
- **[Tuya]** supports a lot more {% term devices %} this release: two-channel DIN rail energy meters with readings per channel, circuit breakers, EV chargers (with their charging current, charging mode, and session energy), water valves (with water usage and temperature), and the keypad tone and alarm push settings of the Duosmart C30 alarm panel. Three-phase electricity meters now also report reactive power, apparent power, and power factor for each phase. Thanks, [@keranm], [@tmunzer], [@o-borovets], [@syl-craft], [@ChayoteJarocho], [@huangrenwei79], and [@epenet]!
- **[Internet Printing Protocol (IPP)]** added page counters, so you can keep track of how many pages, impressions, and sheets your printer has gone through, split into color and black and white on printers that report it. Thanks, [@brianegge]!
- **[ESPHome]** {% term devices %} can now open the connection to Home Assistant themselves, so they work even when Home Assistant can't reach them directly. Turn on the `outgoing_connection` option on your ESPHome device, and Home Assistant takes it from there, with the same encryption as always. Thanks, [@bdraco]!
- **[HomeKit Bridge]** can now show your valve {% term entities %} in the Home app as a faucet, shower head, or sprinkler. How long a valve runs can now also come from any number entity, not just a number {% term helper %}. Thanks, [@louwii17]!
- **[Philips Hue]** added a switch to turn each MotionAware zone on or off, just like you can in the Hue app. Rooms and zones also gained a {% term scene %} select, showing which scene (or smart scene) is currently active, and letting you pick another one. Thanks, [@marcelveldt] and [@sliekens]!
- **[Reolink]** can now control the pan and tilt lens of cameras that combine multiple lenses in one device, when connected to a Reolink <abbr title="network video recorder">NVR</abbr> or Home Hub. Thanks, [@starkillerOG]!
- **[Music Assistant]** announcements can now speak a message, not only play an audio file. The announcement action takes a message and a {% term TTS "text-to-speech" %} entity to speak it, while keeping the pre-announcement chime and announcement volume that `tts.speak` doesn't offer. The AI search tool can now also search inside artists, albums, and playlists. Thanks, [@marcelveldt] and [@balloob]!
- **[Roborock]** wet and dry vacuums now update in real time, so docking or starting a cleaning shows up in Home Assistant right away. The Roborock Q10 can now also clean specific zones, drive to a spot you pick, and report where it is. Thanks, [@piitaya] and [@hmmbob]!
- **[Z-Wave]** added three new {% term conditions %} to the automation editor, with proper fields to fill in: check a device's node status, the value of a configuration parameter, or any other Z-Wave value. Z-Wave {% term triggers %} got the same treatment. Thanks, [@raman325]!
- **[UniFi Network]** now reports the battery level and runtime of your UniFi UPS, along with its output power, current, voltage, and power factor. Thanks, [@jimstrang]!
- **[Broadlink]** now supports the RM5 Plus, which used to fail with a "device not supported" error. Thanks, [@DAB-LABS]!
- **[UniFi Protect]** now supports the key fob, with an {% term "event entity" %} that fires on every button press, and shows each relay input as its own {% term "binary sensor" %}. ### Farewell to the following

Time for a little cleaning, and no, we did not wait for spring! The following {% term "integration" "integrations" %} are no longer available as of this release:

- **Dublin Bus** has been removed. It relied on the Real-Time Passenger Information API, which the National Transport Authority shut down in September 2020, so it has not worked since. For scheduled departures, the [GTFS](/integrations/gtfs/) integration can use the timetable data published by Transport for Ireland.
- **Etherscan** has been removed. It relied on version 1 of the Etherscan API, which has been shut down, so its sensors only showed `False` instead of a balance. To keep tracking a wallet balance, you can use the [RESTful sensor](/integrations/sensor.rest/) with the Etherscan V2 API and your own free API key.
- **Melnor RainCloud** has been removed. Melnor discontinued the original RainCloud WiFi Timer in April 2026, and its cloud service no longer responds.
- **Orange and Rockland Utility (ORU)** has been removed. The endpoint it relied on stopped returning meter data years ago. Orange and Rockland customers can use the [Opower](/integrations/opower/) integration instead.
- **Sigfox** has been removed. It relied on version 1 of the Sigfox backend API, which Sigfox retired.
- **SolarEdge Local** has been removed. It relied on a local web API that SolarEdge disabled in inverter firmware updates starting in 2020. Use the [SolarEdge](/integrations/solaredge/) integration, or the new [SolarEdge Modbus](/integrations/solaredge_modbus/) integration if you want to keep things local.
- **Travis-CI** has been removed. It relied on the travis-ci.org API, which Travis CI shut down in June 2021.
- **Zestimate** has been removed. Zillow retired the API it relied on, and its replacement is aimed at commercial use, so the integration could not simply be updated.

If you still have any of these in your {% term YAML %} configuration, you can remove them.

### A panel for your Modbus connections

### Connect your AI in one click

Home Assistant can act as a [Model Context Protocol](/integrations/mcp_server/) server, which lets AI apps like Claude, ChatGPT, or Cursor talk to your home. Getting there was a bit of a hunt, though: add the {% term integration %}, pick which parts of Home Assistant to expose, and then figure out which URL to hand to your AI app.

The **AI tasks** page under **Settings** > **System** is now called **AI**, and it gained a **Model Context Protocol** card. Turn the server on in a single click, and the card lists the URLs to paste into your AI app, each with a button to copy it. New setups expose everything Home Assistant has to offer, including anything added later, and only allow administrator accounts to connect. You can change both whenever you like, without removing and re-adding the integration.

### Name an entity after its device

Some {% term entities %} are the whole point of their {% term device %}. Calling the smoke sensor of a smoke detector "Smoke detector Smoke detected" doesn't help anyone. Integrations already give their main entity the name of the device, but if you wanted to do the same for another entity, there was no clean way to do it. If you cleared its name, it jumped right back to the name the integration gave it.

The entity settings now have a **Use device name** switch. Turn it on, and the entity goes by the name of its device. The switch also shows you what that device name is, with a link to rename the device if you'd like. Turn it off, and you're back to the entity's own name, filled in with the default from the integration. If you change your mind, a restore button takes you back to the default. ## Backward-incompatible changes

We do our best to avoid making changes to existing functionality that might unexpectedly impact your Home Assistant installation. Unfortunately, sometimes it is inevitable.

We always make sure to document these changes to make the transition as easy as possible for you. This release has the following backward-incompatible changes:

{% details "Authentication" %}

The deprecated legacy mode of the Home Assistant authentication provider has been removed. Usernames are now always handled in their normalized form: lowercase, and without spaces at the start or end. If Home Assistant showed you a repair about usernames that are not normalized, change those usernames to match.

If you have {% term "template" "templates" %} or dashboard cards that rely on the unit of these sensors, update them to handle a count without a unit. The values, entity IDs, and unique IDs are unchanged, so {% term automations %} that only compare the numbers need no changes.

Vehicles that stream their data no longer get the {% term entities %} that can only be updated by {% term polling %}. Updating those required a paid API call, which most people did not expect.

{% details "UpCloud" %}

The option to configure the {% term polling %} interval has been removed from UpCloud. The integration now always polls every 60 seconds. If you had set a longer interval, this means more API calls: a 300 second interval, for example, becomes five times as many calls.

If you need a different update frequency, you can [set your own polling interval](/common-tasks/general/#defining-a-custom-polling-interval).

The **Subscribed to Xbox Game Pass** {% term "binary sensor" %} has been removed, because it never worked. The Xbox API always reported `false`, whether you or your friends were subscribed to Game Pass or not.
